What to watch. Wave three depends on 2027 line reviews at Walmart and pet specialty, which PetWise does not control.

Section 03 · Starting point

PetWise lost share in a growing market, mostly for reasons it can fix.

Net sales have fallen from $243M in 2021 to a forecast $134M in 2026, and the underlying business loses about $4.4M. The causes are pricing, complexity, channel terms and availability, which management controls, plus structural price pressure in commodity items, which it should avoid rather than fight.

Net sales have fallen by almost half since the peak.

Net sales peaked at $243M in 2021 and are forecast at $134M for 2026. The decline steepened in 2024 and 2025 as e-commerce sales fell and accounts trimmed assortments. The 2026 figure includes about $6M of club volume that is unlikely to repeat.

What holds. Store accounts held up better than online: Walmart, Topco and pet specialty still carry most of the volume.

What to watch. The 2026 forecast leans on a second-half e-commerce recovery that had not appeared by October.

Sources: Independent commercial study, 2025; PetWise 2026 Preliminary AOP and 5+7 plan.

Without the tariff refund, PetWise loses money in 2026.

The 2026 forecast shows $0.9M of adjusted EBITDA. That includes a one-time net tariff benefit of $5.3M, so the underlying business loses about $4.4M. Every month from July to December is forecast negative.

What holds. Cost cuts made in 2025 are already in the base: salaried staff fell from 137 to 96 and annualised savings reached about $6.4M.

What to watch. The refund does not repeat, so 2027 starts from the underlying loss, not the reported figure. Collections are also running about $2.5M below the mid-October forecast on weaker Walmart and PetSmart sales.

Sources: PetWise 5+7 plan and appendix; 2026 Preliminary AOP. A-01.

A small core of items makes most sales while the tail loses money.

PetWise sold 5,423 items in FY25. Of these, 549 made 80% of gross sales, while 3,780 items each sold under $10K. 837 items lost money, costing $5.3M of gross margin.

What holds. The core is made of recognisable items: SmartyKat scratchers, Kitty Sift, the Easy Explorer carrier and FurHaven orthopedic beds.

What to watch. Some of the largest items earn little. The top FurHaven sofa bed and the organic catnip display each earned under 4% gross margin.

Sources: PetWise FY25 item-level actuals (May 2024–Apr 2025 file and FY25–26 margin analysis).

Online sales fell fastest at the brands that lost listings and price position.

On Amazon, FurHaven shelf sales fell 49% and SmartyKat 50% between 1 January and 27 August 2026. Most of SmartyKat’s loss came from listings that went dark, led by a catnip shaker that had sold $703K in the same period a year before. Pet Factory held its sales and Treatly grew.

What holds. Brands with repeat purchase, such as Pet Factory and Kitty Sift, held up better than durable beds.

What to watch. FurHaven page visits fell 43% and units per visit slipped from 5.2% to 4.7%, which points to lost search visibility, not price alone.

Sources: PetWise marketplace database (Amazon vendor sales and traffic to 27 Aug 2026). A-27, A-28.

FurHaven sits above the price band where most bed dollars are spent.

Half of Amazon dog-bed sales sit between $30 and $60. FurHaven makes most of its sales above $60 and holds 3.3% of the $30–60 tier. The tier’s best sellers are China-direct brands with tens of thousands of reviews.

What holds. FurHaven’s orthopedic positioning matches the largest search need: orthopedic searches run about nine times calming searches.

What to watch. On Chewy, items whose price rose more than 10% lost 65% of their units, so price moves need elasticity data first.

Sources: Amazon dog-bed subcategory scorecard (Jan–Nov 2025); ASINSIGHT category reports (2026); Chewy State of the Union (Sep 2026).

Dark listings cost Chewy sales about as much as FurHaven’s own decline.

Chewy sales after order discounts fell from $16.5M to $12.4M over the first 35 weeks of 2026. Items that went dark took $2.7M, about the same as the decline in continuing FurHaven items. New items and other continuing brands added $1.3M back.

What holds. Outside FurHaven, continuing items grew about 9% and new items added $0.9M, so the Chewy shelf still responds.

What to watch. Fill rate on Chewy swung between 80% and 94% over the summer, and about 112K units of fourth-quarter forecast had no stock or order behind them.

Sources: Chewy State of the Union, YTD 2026 (Sep 2026); Chewy vendor statements, Jun–Sep 2026.

Budgets assumed growth that the base business did not deliver.

Each annual plan since 2023 set EBITDA well above the outcome. The 2026 plan carried $17.5M of new-business go-gets, 83% of them in the second half. About $3M of the store go-gets arrived and none of the $7M e-commerce go-gets did.

What holds. The 5+7 reforecast reset the base and removed most of the go-gets, which makes it a sounder starting point.

What to watch. A plan built from won business and run-rate actions, tracked weekly, is the remedy this packet proposes.

Sources: PetWise 2023 reforecast, 2024 budget, 2025 AOP, 2026 AOP and 5+7 plan.

Section 04 · Market and competitors

Demand is moving to cat, function and online, where PetWise is under-weight.

The category grows in dollars, not units. The growth is online, in cat and in functional products, while store brands and China-direct sellers own the commodity end. Scaled peers earn double-digit margins with focused categories, fewer sites and disciplined sourcing.

Pet supplies grow in dollars while units stay flat.

NIQ-tracked pet supplies grew 4.8% in dollars in 2025 while units fell 0.5%, so price did the work. In stores, dollars and units both fell, while online grew in both.

What holds. Total US pet spending reached $158B in 2025, up 3.7%, so the category is not shrinking.

What to watch. Durable dog and cat products are forecast to grow about 1% a year, so PetWise must take share to grow.

Sources: NIQ Pet Summit 2026; APPA 2025 industry spend; Packaged Facts durable dog and cat products. A-25.

Most pet supplies are now bought online, and the gap keeps widening.

About 62% of US pet supplies spending is now online. Online grew 17.4% in the year to July 2026 while stores fell 2.5%. Amazon pet sales run about $1.4B a month, up about a third, with third-party sellers more than doubling.

What holds. Chewy hardgoods grew 14.4% in its latest quarter, so a branded supplier can still grow online.

What to watch. About 29% of PetWise’s own sales are online, so the business is positioned against the flow.

Sources: NIQ via GlobalPETS; SmartScout via GlobalPETS; Chewy 10-Q, Q2 FY26. A-25.

Cat is growing faster than dog on Amazon and in households.

Amazon cat products grew 48% over the last year against 16% for dog. Cat households rose 5% to 53M in 2025, and shelters report record cat adoptions while dog adoptions stay below 2016.

What holds. SmartyKat and Kitty Sift already hold shelf space at Walmart, pet specialty and Chewy.

What to watch. Outward Hound’s Catstages line has grown about 36% a year since 2019 and is the most direct branded rival.

Sources: SmartScout via GlobalPETS; APPA; Shelter Animals Count; Outward Hound CIM (2025).

Enrichment and smart products grow fastest within supplies.

Treat-dispensing cameras grew 32%, electronic toys 27% and food dispensers 20% in the year to July 2026. Treat-dispensing toys and lick mats also grew well ahead of the category. Shoppers spend on products that do something for the pet.

What holds. SmartyKat’s Hot Pursuit electronic toy grew 32% on Amazon in 2026, a sign the brand can compete here.

What to watch. Smart feeders and litter devices are moving into PetSmart and other stores, which raises the bar for cat innovation.

Sources: NIQ via GlobalPETS (Sep 2026); PetWise e-commerce board review (Jul 2026).

Store brands and China-direct sellers own the commodity end of the aisle.

Retailer brands hold 85% to 92% of mass-channel dog furniture, crates and litter boxes. On Amazon, China-based sellers hold 67% of cat beds and furniture and 61% of dog crates. Each major account is expanding its own brands in 2026.

What holds. Branded products still win where they are differentiated and well reviewed.

What to watch. Competing on price in commodity items would put PetWise against sellers with lower costs and no brand overhead.

Sources: PLMA and Circana via GlobalPETS; SmartScout via GlobalPETS; retailer filings 2026.

Amazon’s shopping assistant shows a handful of products and favours strong ratings.

Amazon’s assistant narrows a search to about five products and rarely recommends one rated under 4.0 stars. The median pick carries about 3,000 reviews. Sponsored prompts have been billable since March 2026, so the assistant is also an ad channel.

What holds. Gen-AI tools can rebuild attributes, images and answers for a few hundred hero listings in months.

What to watch. FurHaven ran 977 dog-bed listings at about $3K of sales each, which splits reviews and weakens each listing’s chance of being shown.

Sources: PPC Land (Apr 2026); PetWise e-commerce board review (Jul 2026).

Scaled peers earn double-digit margins in the same categories.

Central Garden & Pet’s pet segment earned a 21.4% adjusted EBITDA margin in its latest quarter, and Spectrum’s pet care business about 20% before tariff refunds. Outward Hound, closer in size to PetWise, earns about 11%.

What holds. These peers win with focused categories, fewer sites and disciplined sourcing, which are the same levers in this plan.

What to watch. Peers are also cutting China sourcing and adding automation to logistics, so the bar keeps rising.

Sources: Central Garden & Pet Q3 FY26 call; Spectrum Brands Q3 FY26 8-K; Outward Hound board reporting (2026).

Most of the online decline happened in 2025, and the slide has slowed since.

Amazon shelf sales, which are what shoppers paid, were $18.7M from 1 January to 27 August 2026, against $27.1M a year earlier. Chewy shelf sales over 35 matching weeks were $13.7M against $18.2M. Most of the fall happened during 2025. Since January, Amazon has sold about $70K to $90K a day and Chewy about $340K to $480K a week.

What holds. Chewy has run level since January, and Amazon’s decline has slowed. The gap to last year narrowed from 34% in the first quarter to 21% in the latest 31 days on Amazon, and from 31% to 11% on Chewy.

What to watch. Much of the narrowing comes from easier comparisons. Amazon still drifted down during 2026, from $83K to $90K a day in January and February to about $70K in July and August, and FurHaven was still sliding month by month. The Amazon and Chewy data stop at the end of August, so the October advertising changes are not yet visible.

Sources: PetWise marketplace database (Amazon vendor sales to 27 Aug 2026; Chewy weekly sales to the week of 31 Aug 2026). A-27, A-28.

FurHaven accounts for most of the online decline.

Across Amazon and Chewy, shelf sales fell by $12.9M between the 2025 and 2026 year-to-date periods. FurHaven lost $9.5M of that, or 74%. SmartyKat lost a further $2.1M, most of it from a handful of listings that stopped selling.

What holds. Pet Factory and Treatly grew, goDog was close to level, and new Amazon items added about $0.5M. The decline is concentrated, so the fix can be too.

What to watch. FurHaven fell 49% on Amazon and 35% on Chewy, and it was still down 45% on Amazon in July and August. Until that changes, online sales cannot return to growth.

Sources: PetWise marketplace database (Amazon vendor sales; Chewy weekly sales). A-27, A-28.

Shelf prices rose on FurHaven while what PetWise charges stayed flat.

On the same FurHaven items, Chewy’s shelf price rose about 7% in a year and Amazon’s about 5%. What PetWise charged those retailers moved about 1%. Items carrying about four in ten FurHaven units rose more than 10% at the shelf, and they lost about 60% of their units on both channels.

What holds. On Chewy, FurHaven items whose shelf price stayed within 3% lost about 6% of their units. Where the price held, the volume largely held.

What to watch. On Amazon even flat-priced items lost 44% of their units, so price is not the whole story there. Why shelf prices rose is a question for management: the data cannot tell a higher suggested price from fewer funded promotions or a retailer pricing decision.

Sources: PetWise marketplace database (Amazon vendor sales; Chewy weekly sales). A-30.

Retailers earn little on FurHaven, which helps explain the price moves.

Chewy’s margin on FurHaven, before any allowances PetWise pays, was under 1% in 2025 and about 4% in 2026. It sold more than a third of its FurHaven sales below what it paid. Amazon’s margin on FurHaven fell to about 15% over the summer after it cut shelf prices. On goDog, Sherpa and SmartyKat the same retailers earn 24% to 49%.

What holds. The other brands leave retailers a healthy margin at the shelf, and those brands held up better.

What to watch. Retailers respond to thin margins by raising prices, cutting stock or dropping listings. PetWise pays about 30% of gross sales in allowances on these channels, so there is room to move money from allowances into the invoice price without lowering its own net price.

Sources: PetWise marketplace database (Amazon shipped sales and cost; Chewy net sales and cost); PetWise FY25–26 gross margin analysis for allowance rates. A-27.

Listings that went dark took sales that should be quick to recover.

Five SmartyKat catnip and toy listings sold $1.4M on Amazon in the 2025 period and under $0.1M in 2026. They explain about 80% of SmartyKat’s Amazon decline. The catnip shaker alone sold on 203 days last year and on 28 days this year.

What holds. Demand did not go away. A silvervine catnip item more than doubled on Chewy over the same period, and Hot Pursuit grew 56% on Amazon and 26% on Chewy.

What to watch. Of 215 Amazon items that sold almost daily in 2025, 40 now sell on fewer than half of days, and their sales fell from $1.1M to $0.1M. The database holds no stock data, so it cannot tell an empty shelf from a suppressed listing.

Sources: PetWise marketplace database (Amazon vendor sales; Chewy weekly sales). A-31.

Chewy is growing again outside FurHaven, led by repeat-purchase lines.

Excluding FurHaven, Chewy shelf sales were 20% below last year in the first quarter, 12% below in the second and 12% above in the nine weeks to 31 August. Pet Factory chews grew 24% over the year to date, Kitty Sift 77% and goDog, with its new Tiger Tough line, 14%.

What holds. Autoship, which is Chewy’s subscription program, rose from 16% of PetWise’s Chewy sales in early 2025 to 22% this summer. Pet Factory and Kitty Sift sell more than half of their Chewy volume that way.

What to watch. FurHaven is half of Chewy sales and was still 28% below last year in the latest nine weeks. Two newer lines, Tiger Tough and Treatly, leave Chewy a margin of about 2%, as thin as the margin it earns on FurHaven.

Sources: PetWise marketplace database (Chewy weekly sales to the week of 31 Aug 2026). A-28.

A few hundred items carry most of the online business.

Over the last year, 257 Amazon items sold $25K or more each and made 81% of Amazon shelf sales. On Chewy, 203 items made 76%. At the other end, 1,367 Amazon items and 884 Chewy items sold under $5K each and together made 5% to 6% of sales.

What holds. The hero list is short enough to manage item by item: price, stock, content and retailer margin can be checked weekly for each one.

Section 06 · Commercial

Commercial levers add about $12M by fixing trade terms and backing cat.

Seven commercial initiatives add about $11.9M of run-rate EBITDA at plan. Revenue growth management and retail media are wave-one quick wins; Chewy, the digital shelf and the FurHaven price reset build through 2027; cat growth and key-account programs follow.

Allowances are PetWise’s largest controllable commercial cost.

Discounts and allowances took $25.5M, or 15% of FY25 gross sales. They ran at 31% of gross on e-commerce against 9% in stores, and above 40% at Costco. Revenue growth management, which means managing price, promotion, pack and trade terms together, targets about $3M.

What to watch. Retailers will resist. The plan trades allowance cuts for in-stock and forecast-accuracy commitments rather than asking for them outright. October e-commerce promotions and deductions cost about $690K of cash, and sell-through deductions take 90–120 days to pay back.

Allowances are all discounts and allowances booked against gross sales: retailer programs, co-op, damages, deductions and promotional funding.

Section 07 · Operations

Cutting to about 1,500 active items removes most of the loss-making tail.

PetWise sold 5,423 items in FY25, and 837 of them lost $5.3M of gross margin. Discontinued items still cost $1.5M. Cutting to about 1,500 active items and repricing the remaining loss-makers targets about $2M.

What holds. L.E.K. finds that SKU rationalisation adds 65 to 90 bps of gross margin, and McKinsey cites a case where a 25% cut lifted gross margin by 2 to 4 points.

Should-cost sourcing and redesign can take about 2.5% out of product cost.

Product cost was about $103M in FY25, bought from a fragmented supplier base. Re-quoting top items against should-cost models, consolidating suppliers and redesigning beds and packaging targets about $2.5M. BCG and Inverto put AI-assisted savings at up to 5% of direct spend.

What holds. Management’s own work found real examples: the Nest Top sofa bed’s margin rose from 28% to 35% on a redesigned bill of materials, and foam specifications can fall 12%.

What to watch. Supplier changes take two to three production cycles to land, so savings start in mid-2027.

Sources: PetWise B&M gross margin improvement plan (Aug 2025); BCG and Inverto. A-15.

Findings

The questions the findings raise.

Findings

FurHaven is most of the online decline, and cat earns the best margins.

FurHaven lost $9.5M of the $12.9M fall in online shelf sales (Exhibit 5.02), and its decline tracks shelf-price rises that a terms reset can reverse (Exhibits 5.03 and 6.06). Cat brands earn 23% to 32% gross margin against 20% for FurHaven (Exhibit 6.02), and Amazon cat sales grew about three times as fast as dog (Exhibit 4.03). So the FurHaven reset runs through 2027 to stop the loss, while new investment goes to cat.

On the same items, shelf prices rose far more than PetWise’s prices to the retailers.

FurHaven shelf prices rose about 7% on Chewy and 5% on Amazon, while what the retailers paid PetWise moved about 1% (Exhibit 5.19). The data cannot say why. A higher suggested price, fewer funded promotions and a retailer seeking margin would each look the same, and Chewy earned about 4% on FurHaven before allowances (Exhibit 5.04). Management should confirm the cause before the terms reset is negotiated (A-30).

Because store brands and China-direct sellers set the price floor there.

Retailer brands hold 85% to 92% of mass-channel dog furniture, crates and litter boxes, and China-based sellers hold 61% to 67% of key Amazon categories (Exhibit 4.05). Matching them would mean selling at their cost base without their scale. The plan leaves those items and puts the effort into differentiated, review-rich products.

No: fewer than 600 items make most of the sales, and the tail loses money.

In FY25, 549 items made 80% of gross sales and 837 items lost $5.3M of gross margin (Exhibits 3.03 and 3.15). Online, 1,367 Amazon items made 5% of sales over the last year (Exhibit 5.21). Cuts follow the five tests in Exhibit 7.11, starting with contribution after allowances and freight, so small but profitable items stay (A-13, A-14).

Chewy has stabilised and is growing outside FurHaven, while FurHaven on Amazon is still falling.

Chewy’s gap to last year narrowed from 31% to 11% during 2026, and sales outside FurHaven are 12% above last year (Exhibit 5.06). Chewy hardgoods grew 14.4% in its latest quarter (Exhibit 4.09). Amazon stays important, but is run for profit: about 300 hero listings, ads above breakeven and reset terms (Exhibit 6.12).

Because PetWise’s seller account there sells about $0.3M a year and is shrinking.

Walmart.com and Target.com seller sales total under $1M a year, Walmart.com fell 22% from April to October, and Walmart Fulfillment Services has delivered $12K since March 2025 (Exhibits 5.09 and 5.22). The plan treats both as a capped test with a 2028 target of about $1.8M (Exhibit 6.12, A-10). The larger opportunity on Walmart.com runs through the Walmart buying team and sits in the key-account plan.

Chew manufacturing moves to contract makers, with a written Topco contract agreed first.

Management’s scenarios range from a small transfer to a full shutdown (Exhibit 7.13). The plan favours exiting manufacturing with two contract makers, because the sole rawhide partner is in distress. Topco is the largest customer of the plant, so supply continuity is a condition of the exit, and the risk trigger in Exhibit 9.15 watches its fill rate (A-16). Pet Factory chews also held their Amazon sales and grew on Chewy (Exhibit 5.13), so online supply is part of the same condition.

They can, which is why the plan trades cuts for commitments rather than asking for them outright.

PetWise offers in-stock, forecast-accuracy and content commitments in return for lower allowances on hero items (Exhibits 6.14 and 6.18). If no agreement is reached by the end of the first quarter of 2027, the plan shifts to item-level exits on the accounts concerned (Exhibit 9.15, A-07).
