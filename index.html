Levers PetWise controls take EBITDA to about $7M, and a launch reserve closes the gap.

Fifteen initiatives in three pillars add about $26.2M of run-rate EBITDA at plan, and category erosion, the one-off 2026 club volume, retailer tariff give-backs and reinvestment take back about $7.0M. Weighted for risk, the run-rate reaches about $6.5M by the end of 2028 and 2028 EBITDA is about $6.8M. A launch reserve of about $5M of plan value, sized from management’s 2027 launches and counted at medium confidence, brings the run-rate to about $10M.

Three waves carry the plan case past the target late next year. Wave one is a 90-day sprint on terms, prices, ads, items and overhead, and each wave helps fund the next. Risk-adjusted, the run-rate peaks at about $7.0M in mid-2028, so the plan case passes $10M and the risk-adjusted case does not.

Management’s growth target sits far above the plan’s revenue path. Management aims to double sales to $300M or more by 2030, about 18% a year from 2026. The plan reaches its EBITDA on sales of about $158M in 2028, and management has not yet shown the EBITDA or cash path of its target.

Read the detail · 02 The plan

Sales have fallen by almost half since the peak, mostly for fixable reasons.

Net sales fell from $243M in 2021 to about $132M in 2026, and without the Costco program sales to September fell about 16%. The causes are pricing, complexity, channel terms and availability, which management controls, plus price pressure in commodity items, which the plan avoids rather than fights.

Without the tariff refunds, PetWise loses about $6.1M this year. With actuals to September, 2026 shows about $0.3M of adjusted EBITDA, including $7.4M of one-time tariff refunds less $1.0M of concessions set aside. The refunds do not repeat, so 2027 starts from the underlying loss.

Budgets assumed growth that the base business did not deliver. Each annual plan since 2023 set EBITDA well above the outcome. That record is why this plan runs in waves, with dated gates and weekly measures.

Read the detail · 03 Starting point

Demand is moving to cat, function and online, where PetWise is under-weight.

NIQ-tracked pet supplies grew 4.8% in dollars in 2025 while units fell 0.5%, so price did the work. In stores, dollars and units both fell, while online grew in both. Store brands and China-direct sellers hold the commodity end of the aisle, and scaled peers earn double-digit margins.

Cat is growing faster than dog on Amazon and in households. Amazon cat products grew 48% over the last year against 16% for dog, and cat households rose 5% to 53M in 2025. SmartyKat and Kitty Sift already hold shelf space at Walmart, pet specialty and Chewy.

Read the detail · 04 Market and peers

PetWise brands hold real advantages, but rivals now beat each on something shoppers see.

Each brand is scored against named rivals on price, rating, review depth, Amazon search, price consistency and a provable claim. Kitty Sift scores 25 of 30 and FurHaven and goDog 16, and Amazon search is the common gap. Online-native sellers, retailer own brands and branded specialists each press on a different PetWise brand.

FurHaven carries a price premium that its rating does not support. On Chewy, FurHaven’s large orthopedic sofa sells about 22% above Bedsure’s with a rating 0.2 stars lower, and goDog’s dragon sits about 22% above Chewy’s own plush. The same FurHaven sofa model and size sold for $24.08 at Walmart and $57.21 on Chewy on the same day.

Read the detail · 05 Competition and positioning

Online shelf sales fell by more than a quarter, mostly at FurHaven.

Across Amazon and Chewy, shelf sales, which are what shoppers paid, fell by $12.9M between the 2025 and 2026 year-to-date periods. FurHaven lost $9.5M of that, or 74%, and SmartyKat lost a further $2.1M, most of it from a handful of listings that stopped selling.

Shelf prices rose on FurHaven while what PetWise charges stayed flat. On the same FurHaven items, Chewy’s shelf price rose about 7% in a year and Amazon’s about 5%, while what PetWise charged moved about 1%. Items priced up more than 10% lost about 60% of their units on both channels.

Chewy is growing again outside FurHaven, led by repeat-purchase lines. Excluding FurHaven, Chewy shelf sales moved from 20% below last year in the first quarter to 12% above in the nine weeks to 31 August. Pet Factory and Kitty Sift sell more than half of their Chewy volume through Autoship, Chewy’s subscription program.

Read the detail · 06 Marketplace evidence

Store accounts still carry most of PetWise’s sales, though they shrink each year.

Store accounts bought $117.3M of PetWise’s $166.5M of gross sales in FY25, and the 2026 forecast puts them 25% below 2023. Store allowances run far below online ones, so the store business is cheaper to serve. The store shelf shares here run to August 2024 and will be replaced when current scan data arrives.

Retailer own brands hold more than half the store shelf in beds and carriers. In January to August 2024 they held 61% of pet bedding sold in stores and 52% of carriers, and above 80% of bedding at Walmart and in the pet specialty chains. Walmart’s own-brand bedding includes Vibrant Life beds that PetWise makes.

Walmart has held while most other store accounts shrank. Walmart carries 38% of store sales and stayed within 6% of its 2023 level. Target fell 72% after it moved pet products to its own brand, and Costco fell because its yearly program moved into early 2026.

Read the detail · 07 Store evidence

Commercial value starts with trade terms, where allowances vary widely by account.

Seven commercial initiatives add about $11.9M of run-rate EBITDA at plan. Discounts and allowances took $25.5M, or 15% of FY25 gross sales, at 31% of gross on e-commerce against 9% in stores. Revenue growth management and retail media are the wave-one quick wins, and cat growth and key-account programs follow. Retailers now want part of the tariff refund back, which the plan counts as about $1M a year of give-backs.

Paid media runs below breakeven at Walmart, Amazon and Chewy. In October to date ads returned 4.31x at Walmart, 3.30x at Amazon and 3.26x at Chewy, against breakevens of about 12.2x, 4.6x and 5.6x. Breakeven counts ad-attributed sales, so the shortfall is a floor on direct return.

Stopping the named cut list delivers most of the lever. 73 items under 60% of breakeven are worth about $0.9M a year against the lever’s $1.0M. The simulator on the Commercial tab moves the cut list, returns and spend, and these are its four cases.

Read the detail · 08 Commercial

Operations levers add about $12M by simplifying what PetWise makes and moves.

Six operations initiatives add about $12.4M of run-rate EBITDA at plan, led by sourcing and the site footprint. PetWise sold 5,423 items in FY25, and 837 of them lost $5.3M of gross margin, so item cuts start in the first 90 days. Supplier changes take two to three production cycles, so sourcing savings start in mid-2027.

Chewy fill rate has swung far below the level the plan requires. Chewy fill rate was 74% in late September 2025 and 86% in late September 2026, after an 80% to 94% swing over the summer. Low stock on key items suppresses Chewy’s search ranking, which compounds the lost sales.

Read the detail · 09 Operations

AI use cases enable about a third of the plan’s gross value.

Ten use cases, from demand forecasting to deduction recovery, enable about $10M of the $26M gross plan, counted inside the initiatives they support. Most use proven tools rather than custom models, and the fastest pay back within a quarter.

PetWise cannot yet see profit by item, and that gap comes first. Standard costs carried current tariffs on about 300 of 3,300+ items in 2026, and two ERPs run side by side. The first 90 days build the item-level view that pricing, item and ad decisions need.

Read the detail · 10 Technology and AI

Without a launch reserve, the plan has about a 5% chance of its target.

Ten thousand simulated runs vary lever size, delivery, timing, erosion, give-backs and the starting point. The middle outcome for 2028 is about $6.1M, and eight in ten runs fall between about $3.0M and $9.2M. About 52% of runs reach $6M or more, against a loss of about $6.1M today. A launch reserve of about $5M lifts the chance of $10M to about 37%, and ten more points of capture on top to about 67%.

The downside case still earns well above today, and plan delivery clears the target. The downside case gives about $3.3M in 2028, the base case about $6.8M and the plan delivered in full about $13.3M. The simulator on the Roadmap tab loads these three cases and moves delivery, timing and erosion.

Freed inventory covers the one-off costs, but not the product transitions. Cutting items and days of supply releases about $7–8M of inventory cash in 2027, against about $4.5M of one-off costs. Management estimates about $15M of cash for product transitions by the end of the first quarter of 2027, not yet sized line by line.

Section 02 · The plan

The plan rests on levers PetWise controls, sized from its own data.

Fifteen initiatives across three pillars add about $26M of gross EBITDA improvement. Net of headwinds and weighted for risk, 2028 EBITDA is about $6.8M, and a launch reserve of about $5M of plan value, sized from the 2027 launches, is needed to reach $10M. The plan case passes a $10M run-rate in the fourth quarter of 2027. This tab sets out the pillars, the value bridge, the pace, the scorecard, the risks and the decisions needed now.

Most of the value sits in levers that management controls directly.

The 2026 business loses about $6.1M before the one-time tariff refunds. Fifteen initiatives in three pillars take EBITDA most of the way to the target, and most of the gain comes from margin and cost rather than new volume, so the outcome depends more on execution than on the market.

What holds. More than half of the value sits in trade terms, items, sourcing and landed cost, plants and sites, which PetWise can change without waiting for a retailer.

What to watch. The growth levers depend on retailers and line reviews. At half of plan, cat growth, the FurHaven reset and key-account programs would remove about $2.9M from the full-potential case.

Section 03 · Starting point

PetWise lost share in a growing market, mostly for reasons it can fix.

Net sales have fallen from $243M in 2021 to about $132M in 2026, and the underlying business loses about $6.1M before the one-time tariff refunds. Net sales peaked at $243M in 2021 and are about $132M for 2026 on the latest view, with actuals to September and the last quarter at forecast. What holds. Store accounts held up better than online: Walmart, Topco and pet specialty still carry most of the volume, and Walmart was about 1% above last year to September.

What to watch. The 2026 forecast leaned on a second-half e-commerce recovery that had not appeared by October. In September e-commerce shipped $4.1M against $6.3M forecast, and PetWise brands’ sales at Walmart fell 6.7% in the latest four weeks, with stores down 9.0% (Exhibit 3.23).

Without Costco, sales to September fell about 16%. Costco bought $11.2M of goDog toys to September against $1.5M a year before, which flatters the total. Without it, store accounts fell about 13% and e-commerce about 24%, so the underlying decline is about twice the reported 8%.

Note · what counts as allowances

Allowances are all discounts and allowances booked against gross sales: retailer programs, co-op, damages, deductions and promotional funding.

Assumption · size of the prize

About 6% better use of about $25M of allowances gives about $1.5M. Recovering invalid deductions adds about $0.6M and price and mix about $0.9M, for about $3.0M in all (A-07, A-08).

Method · how levers are sized

Method · marketplace data

Marketplace figures are what shoppers bought at shelf prices. Year-to-date comparisons use the same days for Amazon and matching weeks for Chewy, and same-item price measures use 2025 units as weights (A-27, A-28, A-30).

Method · scope

The packet covers operating levers only. Every lever is a change PetWise makes in how it sells, makes, moves and runs the business.

Method · risk weighting

High, medium and low-confidence levers count at 90%, 70% and 50% of plan value (A-03). Headwinds and reinvestment are counted in full.

Method · simulation

The simulation runs the plan model 10,000 times with a fixed seed, drawing lever size, capture, timing, erosion, retailer give-backs and the starting point each time. Competitor prices, ratings and rating counts were read from public retailer pages, most on 10 November 2026, one like-for-like hero item per category. Amazon figures are third-party estimates and dog-bed shares are from early 2025 (A-38, A-39).

Method · paid media read

Paid media figures come from PetWise’s paid media dashboard, with data to 26 October 2026. Breakeven is realized price over pre-ad contribution margin per unit, so it counts ad-attributed sales and leaves out halo. The dashboard’s source files were not read for this packet (A-43 to A-46).

Store-account sales and margins come from PetWise’s operating plans and margin files, at invoice prices.
