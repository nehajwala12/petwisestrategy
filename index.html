Section 01 · The plan on a page

Fifteen stores carry most of the decline, and the fastest fixes cost nothing.

Fat Tuesday's comparable stores were −7.7% on last year over the twelve weeks to 8 November 2026, and −13.5% over the last four. This plan is built from the company's own Toast, ADP, door-scan and email data, set against the market and management's response of 27 October, and this version answers management's two points on labour and Las Vegas pricing. The loss is guests at fifteen stores, and controls and stock come first.

Over twelve weeks the fifteen stores where the opportunity sits were −14.8% and the other 61 −2.8%. The loss is guests rather than spend. The fastest levers are settings and stock: discount approvals, void controls, add-on targets and the souvenir cups that were missing this October. Together with store plans and the holiday season, the plan is worth about 4.6 comp points at its risk-adjusted value.

What holds. Most of the plan needs decisions rather than capital, so it can start on 9 November.

What to watch. The last four weeks ran −13.5% and the week of 2 November −12.1%, so the plan starts from a weaker run-rate than the summer.

Section 03 · Where sales stand

Sales are still below last year, and the gap sits mostly in fifteen stores.

Same-store sales are compared week for week with last year, on Toast item-level net sales, for the 76 comparable stores. This tab sets out the three windows, the weekly trend, the latest four weeks, the regions, three years of monthly sales and the stores not yet in the base.

Sales are below last year in each window, and the last four weeks were weakest.

Over the twelve weeks to 8 November, sales were −7.7% on last year, a shortfall of $1.1M. The fifteen stores where the opportunity sits were −14.8% and carried 79% of it; the other 61 were −2.8%. The last four weeks were weaker, at −13.5%, because mid-October fell away and November has opened near −14%.

What holds. The other 61 stores are −2.8% over twelve weeks, so the decline is still concentrated rather than estate-wide.

What to watch. The last four weeks: the other 61 slipped to −6.5%, so October's weakness has reached beyond the fifteen.

Sources: Toast POS item-level sales to 8 Nov 2026; Toast order data for checks.

Weekly sales ran below last year in most weeks of the year.

45 of the last 52 weeks were below the same week a year earlier. The average weekly comp was −8.1%, and most weeks sat within about 15 points of it. The swings are mostly calendar: Labor Day fell in a different week this year, and October 2025 carried game days and a Halloween cup that 2026 did not.

What holds. Most weeks stayed inside the band; the decline is steady rather than accelerating.

What to watch. The two weeks below the band, late February and early March, are the Mardi Gras shift. The week of 12 October, at −20.3%, came close to the lower edge.

Sources: Toast POS item-level sales, 52 weeks to 8 Nov 2026.

The other 61 stores recovered most of the way, while the fifteen did not.

Across the year the other 61 moved from −8.5% in the first quarter to −2.5% in the latest one. The fifteen stayed between −16% and −10% in every quarter. That gap is why the plan concentrates on fifteen stores before it asks anything of the rest.

What holds. The improvement in the other 61 is their own run-rate, so the plan is measured against it, not against last year alone.

What to watch. The latest four-week block put the other 61 back at −6.5%, so their recovery is not yet secure.

Sources: Toast POS item-level sales, 52 weeks to 8 Nov 2026.

The last four weeks ran about −14%, and November has opened at the same pace.

Over the four weeks 12 Oct–8 Nov the estate was −13.5%, −$603K. The fifteen were −23.3% and carried 72% of the loss on about 42% of sales, against 79% over twelve weeks, because the other 61 slipped from −2.8% to −6.5%. In Las Vegas part of the slip is the calendar: last year's When We Were Young festival filled 17–19 October and did not run this year, and the matching four days explain $72K of the Vegas shortfall of $275K; leaving them out, Vegas stores off the list were −10.2% and the stores on the list −20.1%. The week of 2 November was −12.1%, with Las Vegas −15%, Florida −13%, Arizona −18% and Louisiana −7%. October closed at −10.6% on the same days last year.

What holds. The fifteen fell further than the rest in every week, and Glendale, Tempe, Project 63 and Neonopolis were the largest losses in the latest week. The plan's levers do not depend on the market turning.

What to watch. Las Vegas off the list has now run about −10% for three weeks after the festival lap, worst around the centre Strip; last October's seasonal cups are the first thing to check, and the Levers tab shows they were not on the shelf. Any eight-week figure quoted should run to the latest Sunday: the eight weeks to 8 Nov are about −11% for the estate.

Sources: Toast POS daily item-level sales, 12 Oct–8 Nov 2026 and the same days of 2025; festival dates from the organisers.

Outside the fifteen, Las Vegas and Florida were above last year over twelve weeks.

Las Vegas was −5.3% over twelve weeks, and +1.1% outside its 5 stores among the fifteen. Florida and the Southeast were −7.5%, or −2.7% outside the fifteen. Louisiana was −7.9% with three of the fifteen, so its decline is broad rather than concentrated.

What holds. Las Vegas and Florida stores outside the fifteen show that the markets themselves can still grow.

What to watch. Louisiana is the exception: the rest of the region was −5.8%, so weekday trade and the neighbourhood stores need their own plan.

Sources: Toast POS item-level sales to 8 Nov 2026.

The loss is fewer guests, while the average check held up.

Over twelve weeks the estate lost about $1.1M from fewer checks and gained about $48K from a higher average check. The pattern is the same at the fifteen. 16 stores grew, and the two largest gains, Grand Bazaar and Venetian 2, are in Las Vegas, the same market as several of the largest losses.

What holds. With price per item flat, the higher check comes from more items and add-ons; the task is getting guests back through the door.

What to watch. Part of the check gain is price per item, which rises with every discount the stores stop giving, so it should be read with the discount rate.

Sources: Toast POS item-level sales and order counts, twelve weeks to 8 Nov 2026.

Last year fell in each month, so this year laps a soft base.

On the 71 stores that traded every month since January 2024, 2025 sales were −10.8% on 2024. This year has been flat in January, February and September, and down in the other months. On a two-year basis August to October sit about 18% below 2024.

What holds. November and December 2025 were −13% and −12% on 2024, so the holidays lap a weak year.

What to watch. If the two-year stack holds, November and December would land near −6% and −7% before any plan, which sets the starting point for the Q4 target.

Sources: Toast POS monthly item-level sales, January 2024 to October 2026.

Gaslamp is above its opening year again, and Downtown FLL joins the base well below it.

Gaslamp was −8.4% over twelve weeks and +9.7% over the last four, against its opening year. Downtown FLL was −26.2%. The base is fixed for all three windows at the stores eligible on 3 August; Downtown FLL reached eighteen months on 22 August, so it is shown here and joins the base from November. DT Jacksonville opened on 5 October and rang no sales after 17 October, which the company should confirm.

What holds. At its latest four-week pace Gaslamp would lift the comp when it joins on 22 December; at its twelve-week pace it would cost about 0.4 points.

What to watch. Downtown FLL is about a quarter below its first year and joins the base when the twelve-week window starts after its eighteenth month, on 16 November, which costs the comp about 0.3 points.

Sources: Toast POS item-level sales to 8 Nov 2026.

Section 04 · Against the outside world

Peers are soft too, but Fat Tuesday's own stores show the markets can still grow.

This tab sets Fat Tuesday against listed peers, the consumer backdrop and the indicators for Las Vegas, New Orleans and Florida, then against its own stores in the same markets. Outside figures were refreshed on 29 October 2026 and each is dated where it is used.

Most beverage and destination peers are soft, and food-led chains are growing.

Fat Tuesday's twelve-week comp of −7.7% sits close to Ark Restaurants and below Dave & Buster's. Chains that lead with food, Chili's and Darden among them, are growing. RCI's Bombshells grew after repositioning its food and drink, which is the closest public example of a bar concept turning its own traffic.

What holds. Fat Tuesday's decline is in line with its nearest peers rather than an outlier.

What to watch. The growing chains are winning on value and food, which is where Fat Tuesday is weakest.

Sources: SEC filings and company releases to 29 Oct 2026; Toast POS to 8 Nov 2026.

Las Vegas visitors are flat, and Fat Tuesday's stores split by corridor.

Visitor numbers are close to last year, but room rates and airport traffic are falling and both large operators report lower Strip EBITDA. Inside that market, Fat Tuesday's Vegas stores ranged from −17% to +15% over twelve weeks. In October nearly every Vegas store fell, which points to something they share, such as the cups missing from the shelf, more than to the market.

What holds. Six of the fourteen Vegas stores grew over twelve weeks, so the market is not shut to growth.

What to watch. The F1 race on 19–21 November closes parts of the Strip; corridor plans for that weekend should be in place before it.

Sources: LVCVA, Nevada Gaming Control Board, Clark County, MGM and Caesars releases to 29 Oct 2026; Toast POS to 8 Nov 2026.

New Orleans is soft, and Florida's stores split as widely as Las Vegas.

New Orleans airport traffic and hotel revenue are down on last year, in line with management's own read. Louisiana's decline is spread across all five area groups rather than concentrated. In Florida and the Southeast the stores range widely, with Rivercenter and South Bank in San Antonio among the best in the estate and the two Bayside stores among the weakest.

What holds. Florida's total visitor count is flat, so the Florida stores that fell are losing share of a steady market.

What to watch. Canadian visitors to Florida are down by about a seventh; the winter season will show whether that deepens.

Sources: Airport statistics, CoStar, VISIT FLORIDA, Placer via KRVS; Toast POS.

Fat Tuesday has held its prices while costs and competitors' menus rise.

Net sales per item were −1.3% on last year over twelve weeks, while drinks away from home rose about 2.6% and hospitality wages about 3.9%. The share of adults who drink is flat on last year, at the low of Gallup's series. Holding prices protects traffic, but it means discounts and voids come straight out of a margin that is not being repriced.

What holds. Flat prices leave room for a tested increase on the cups where Fat Tuesday is cheapest, once the controls are in.

What to watch. Consumer sentiment is still low, so a price move should be tested at matched stores before it is rolled out.

Sources: BLS CPI, Conference Board, University of Michigan, Gallup, Federal Reserve; Toast POS.

The holidays and Mardi Gras will move the comp more than the market will.

The next four months hold the F1 race, Thanksgiving, the National Finals Rodeo, Christmas and New Year, CES and an earlier Mardi Gras. Each lands in a known week, so each can carry a store target. Downtown FLL joins the comparable base from November and Gaslamp on 22 December.

What holds. None of these is a surprise; the stores can plan cups, gallons and staffing for each one.

What to watch. Mardi Gras falls on 9 February 2027 against 17 February this year, so early February will compare well and mid-February badly; March then laps a weak month.

Sources: Event organisers; Toast POS.

About half of the weekly note's October points hold up against the data.

The research log lists what could not be found, and the October fact-check now includes the two items that needed Toast's daily data.

Sources: CreditSwan research log; company weekly note of 19–20 Oct; NWS; Toast POS.

Section 05 · The fifteen stores where the opportunity sits

Fifteen stores carry most of the shortfall, and most still earn good margins.

The fifteen are the comparable stores with the largest twelve-week dollar shortfalls to 8 November. This tab ranks them, sets each against its local peers and its own area traffic, shows how and when each lost sales, what is worth saving, management's view of each, and a card per store with a first move.

The fifteen stores span four markets and carry most of the shortfall.

The fifteen lost $825K over twelve weeks, 79% of the estate's shortfall. Five are in Las Vegas, four in Florida and the Southeast, four in Louisiana and two in Arizona. Collins Ave, Forum 1 and Riverwalk joined the list on the fresh window; Resorts World closed, and Philadelphia and Stockyards moved off it.

What holds. 15 of the fifteen were also below last year over 52 weeks, so this is not a one-quarter list.

What to watch. The bottom of the list is close: the next six stores, led by Forum 1 and Riverwalk, sit within about $6K of fifteenth place.

Sources: Toast POS item-level sales to 8 Nov 2026; company store EBITDA file.

Each store trails its own peers, and area traffic explains part of the gap.

Against the other stores in the same area group, every one of the fifteen is behind; Glendale and Tempe, with no other Arizona stores, are set against the other 61. Management's Placer figures add a second test: at the eleven stores with a figure, the fall in area foot traffic lines up with about 60% of the 52-week shortfall. The rest, about $1.3M, is store-specific and sits mostly at Ybor, Aladdin 2, Tempe, Bayside 1 and Mandalay Bay.

What holds. Project 63, Neonopolis, Glendale and Bourbon track their blocks, so their plans are about share of a smaller crowd.

What to watch. Tempe is 40 points behind its own block, which grew; that is the clearest store-specific gap on the list.

Sources: Toast POS; Placer.ai figures in management's response of 27 Oct 2026.

Section 06 · Who the guests are and who comes back

The guest data is flowing again, and it shows locals are the base worth keeping.

Two guest sources are live again in the database: the ID scans taken at the door and the Toast email list. This tab sets out what each now covers, what the scans say about who comes back, who each store serves, how fast the email list is growing and what to do with it.

The guest feeds are running again, though door scanning has thinned.

Local guests come back several times as often as visitors.

What holds. The pattern is the same in every cohort, so it does not depend on one season or one store.

What to watch. Next-month return among new locals has fallen since early 2025. Some of that is scanners going quiet, so it needs re-reading once scanning is restored.

Sources: ID_Scan table, guests first scanned January 2025 to September 2026.

The gap between Las Vegas stores is add-ons, not price, and the programme is working.

The method reads the company's own systems and names every estimate.

What the plan does, how it measures, and where the data stops.

Method · source systems

Sales, checks, items, discounts, voids, hours, door scans and email addresses are read from the company's Toast and ADP data, not from management reports. Where a management figure is used it is named as management's.

Method · comparable sales

Each store is compared with itself on the same weekdays a year earlier. Stores with gaps use weeks where both years traded. Chef Hwy's Jazz Fest fortnight is removed both years.

Method · lever sizing

Each lever is sized on the twelve weeks to 8 November (discounts, voids, labour, traffic) or to 25 October (cups, refills, floaters per daiquiri) as the gap to last year or to the market's best quarter, converted at a confidence and reduced for overlap.

Method · what the plan does not do

It is not a quality-of-earnings review, a valuation or a financing proposal. It verifies the commercial engine and sizes the recovery from the company's own data.

Watch · data gaps

Hourly labour for five stores is not in ADP this year; Toast holds no COGS or rent; Placer periods are not stated; the 2026 P&L has not been provided.

Source · pull dates

Toast queried 9 Nov 2026, sales and checks to 8 Nov; ADP read to 1 Nov because the latest week's timecards are not yet complete; item-mix and guest data to 25–28 Oct; outside data refreshed 29 Oct 2026.

Method · guest data

Door scans are mapped to stores by scanner name, with seven corrections. A local guest is one whose licence state matches the store's state. Email capture counts an address once, at its first order.

Method · prices and add-ons

The item table stores each cup's gross price with its floater and extra-shot modifiers on the same line. Menu prices are therefore read as the lowest unit price rung for each cup, and everything above that is counted as an add-on. Checked store by store, every Las Vegas store charges the same shelf price; the earlier price table on the Strip was add-ons, and the price lever is withdrawn.

Method · labour by hour

ADP clock-ins are stored on a 12-hour clock. Shifts starting at 1–7 o'clock are read as afternoon and 8–12 as morning, which recovers the number of people on the clock each hour to within an hour and lets weekday rotas be compared with weekday sales store by store.

Method · simulation

The simulated range draws each lever around its confidence 20,000 times with a shared delivery factor. The interactive simulators use the same lever values.
